Executive Summary
Preliminary · Subject to revision

Meridian IR has completed sufficient forensic analysis to confirm the initial attack vector and resolve the insider access question flagged earlier in this engagement. This preliminary findings report communicates those two conclusions ahead of our full written report, which will follow within 48 hours.

The attack originated from an unpatched vulnerability on an externally-facing VPN appliance. The threat actor exploited this vulnerability approximately three weeks before ransomware was deployed, establishing persistent access and conducting reconnaissance — including credential theft — during that dwell period. The employee flagged by anomalous access logs has been cleared. The access was attributable to the threat actor operating under a compromised credential, not to any action by the employee.

Patch Window — Critical Finding
Confirmed
47

Patch available before
exploitation
A patch for the exploited VPN vulnerability was publicly available for 47 days before the threat actor used it to enter your network.
The vulnerability was disclosed and patched by the vendor on 11 March 2026. Sawatch Health System's VPN appliance remained unpatched at the time of exploitation on approximately 28 April 2026 — 47 days later. The patch was not applied before the threat actor identified and exploited the vulnerability.
Implication for this engagement
This finding will be material to any regulatory inquiry, litigation, and breach notification. Sawatch Health System's patch management process for externally-facing systems will require documented review. The 47-day window should be addressed directly with legal counsel before any external communication referencing root cause.

04
Insider Allegation — Resolution
Employee Cleared
✓ Finding confirmed — employee cleared
The anomalous access pattern flagged earlier in this engagement — large-volume patient record queries outside normal working hours from a privileged account — has been attributed to the threat actor operating under a compromised credential, not to the account holder. The employee had no involvement in the breach. Their credential was stolen during the threat actor's dwell period as part of a broader credential harvesting operation across Active Directory.

The employee should be informed of this finding through appropriate HR and legal channels at the earliest opportunity.
Implications for the organisation
  • Any suspension, investigation, or HR action taken against the employee based solely on the access log indicators should be reviewed with legal counsel immediately. The employee has not been found to have acted improperly.
  • The organisation has an obligation to notify the employee clearly and promptly that they have been cleared. Delay in doing so creates additional legal and reputational exposure.
  • The credential compromise that enabled this access pattern suggests that other privileged credentials obtained during the dwell period may still be active or in use. Credential hygiene across the environment should be treated as an ongoing containment priority, not a completed action.
  • This finding does not resolve the question of whether a separate insider threat exists. It resolves this specific allegation only. Meridian IR's full report will address the broader insider risk posture separately.
